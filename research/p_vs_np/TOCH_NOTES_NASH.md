# Nash 1955 -> DemiHead notes

The usable JANUS lesson is not "2^r keys therefore exponential hardness." Nash explicitly noticed that simple structures can permit much shorter shortcuts. The research question is therefore the cost of the **best structure-preserving shortcut**.

For DemiHead this becomes:

- candidate count is never a lower bound;
- interaction complexity is only a hypothesis until connected to a proved obstruction;
- representation discovery must search for exact shortcuts before deeper branching;
- failure to find a shortcut remains `OPEN`;
- any claimed universal shortcut must pay discovery, representation, nested transition, certificate, and verification costs.

`P_VS_NP = OPEN`.
